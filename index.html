METHODOLOGY

Scientific basis behind the simulator

This tool uses an educational rule system based on well-known perception and UX principles. It is not webcam eye tracking and does not measure a real user’s gaze.

1) Task-based scan flow

For product pages, people need a usable sequence: identify the item, understand the offer, evaluate price, choose options, then act. When this sequence is broken, friction increases.

2) Gestalt grouping

Related items are easier to understand when they are spatially close and visually grouped. Price should feel attached to the product details; size chart should feel attached to variants.

3) Visual crowding & occlusion

When items overlap or compete in a cramped space, recognition becomes harder. Critical content that is obscured cannot earn a strong score.

4) Spatial expectations

Shoppers carry learned expectations: price near the title, options before add to cart, and reassurance close to the decision zone.

5) Initial viewport / above-the-fold

Elements shown before scrolling are easier to discover early. This does not mean everything must be at the top, but core decision items should not be buried.

6) Simulated heatmap

The heatmap is a weighted visualization based on visibility, position, task importance, and grouping. It is a heuristic illustration, not a biometric reading.

References used for the in-app recommendations

Gestalt grouping, visual crowding research, above-the-fold attention, information scent / task support, and layout heuristics such as Gutenberg for comparison only.
